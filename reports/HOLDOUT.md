# Holdout test: the macro slope on new markets

Status at 13 October 2026 00:10 UTC: **blinded**. Regenerated by `calatlas holdout` (downloads new markets) or `calatlas report` (from the saved data).

## The claim under test

In the original sample, finance and macro markets priced 7 days before their scheduled end had a recalibration slope of **b = 1.66** (1.21 to 2.10), from 471 markets in 65 events; partial pooling shrank it to 1.29. That pattern was found after looking at the data, so it is tested here on markets that **closed after the collection**, under rules frozen on 13 October 2026 ([PROTOCOL.md](../PROTOCOL.md)).

## Pre-registered design

- **New data only**: markets that closed after 12 October 2026 06:22 UTC.
- **Primary population**: events with at least $5,769,105 traded, the size of the smallest event in the original sample. **Secondary**: every event, markets still need $10,000 each.
- **Read once**, on the first 65 primary events to close (the original's size); until then this page shows counts, never the slope. If that count is not reached by 31 December 2027, the test is read on what there is and labelled underpowered.
- **Verdict**: *supported* if the one sided 95% lower bound on b is above 1; *reversed* if the two sided 95% interval lies below 1; otherwise *not supported*. Errors are clustered by event.
- **Power**: about 90% if the true slope is the original 1.66, and about 36% if it is the pooled 1.29. A *not supported* verdict mostly rules out an effect as large as first measured; it is weak evidence that macro prices are calibrated.
